IHSG & Sectoral Stock Analysis

Data Visualization · Capital Market Analysis

Python Streamlit Pandas Plotly

About this project

Five representative stocks were selected from different sectors to compare how industry characteristics affect investment performance and risk in different ways The stocks analyzed in this project: Financial Sector: BBCA (BCA) Consumer Sector: UNVR (Unilever) Mining Sector: ADRO (Adaro) Infrastructure: TLKM (Telkom) Real Estate Sector: BSDE (BSD City) and the IHSG as the benchmark

What I Did

  • Data collection and preprocessing : Closing price from yfinance library from 2020-2025
  • Exploratory data analysis
  • Statistical modeling and visualization
  • Interpretation of results and business insights

Findings & Results

The most fascinating insight was the correlation breakdown, i identified that during COVID-19 shock, inter-sector diversification potential almost vanished as sectors began moving in sync By constructing correlation and analyzing rolling volatility, i found that diversification is often an illusion during market stress. The movement of the IHSG is heavily influenced by the financial and telecommunications sectors. The financial sector correlates fairly evenly with all other sectors, indicating that the financial sector (BBCA) acts as a link between sectors in the economy. Diversifying into sectors with low correlation to BBCA (such as consumer or mining) can serve as a risk mitigation strategy. The best pair for diversification : Consumer & Mining sector (correlation = 0,148)

Flood Risk Analysis

Statistical Modeling

R Statistical Modeling Ordinal Regression Environmental Risk

About This Project

Coastal flooding is a growing impact of climate change that increases risk in coastal areas. Since flood risk is categorized into ordered levels (low, moderate, high), an appropriate statistical approach such as ordinal logistic regression is needed to identify the key influencing factors.

What I Did

  • Collected Coastal Climate Risk Dataset (1,653 observations).
  • Standardized numerical variables (Z-score normalization).
  • Tested assumptions (multicollinearity and proportional odds).
  • Applied partial proportional odds model due to assumption violations.
  • Performed backward selection using AIC and likelihood ratio test.
  • Evaluated model fit and parameter significance.

Findings & Results

Elevation, average precipitation, low drainage capacity, coastal distance, and historical flood events significantly influence coastal flood risk levels. The final model is statistically significant and effectively explains the variation in flood risk.
